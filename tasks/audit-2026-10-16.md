**The three things that cost the most results:**

1. **No email is ever really verified.** The SMTP check can't run because Vercel and Supabase block port 25, so it passes every address through. 156 of the 178 first emails went to scraped addresses that were never checked. That is the main reason bounces are 6%+ (§3, P0-2).
2. **The model still invents statistics,** and the gate that catches it is switched off. "That's probably costing you 20–30% of inbound requests" and "typically cut content time by 60–70%" were sent a week *after* the prompt was told to stop. 31 of the 40 gate holds are exactly this, and the gate was right every time.
3. **One wrong-name email lost a willing buyer.** Acadian replied: *"Yes I am interested in having a company do this… but I can't imagine paying a company that doesn't get my company name correct and even gets the website url wrong."* That one email is the best argument for building "never send garbage" before automating.

**What worked:** the only positive reply (GYMVMT) came through the **free audit tool**. Our email went out at 20:27, they ran the audit at 22:39, then replied *"did the audit and interested."* The audit CTA is the one mechanism with proof. The learning loop should be built around it.

**Blockers before any automation:**
- **Legal (CASL):** emails have no unsubscribe link, no List-Unsubscribe header and no mailing address.
- **Security:** four tables are writable by anyone holding the public anon key. That includes the warmup templates the engine sends from `pedro@runnareach.com`.

**"Learning" doesn't exist yet.** The /learning page is a shell. Prompts are hard-coded, nothing records which prompt wrote which email, and no outcome changes anything. Most of the tables a learning loop needs (`prompts`, `prompt_variants`, `prompt_change_proposals`, `opportunities`, `pitch_events`, pgvector) already exist in the schema but are empty or unused.

### P1: quality and targeting (hurts results, not legal or safety)

- **Wrong-company domains.** Discovery stores the listing's domain verbatim (`crawl-action.ts:225`). The ownership check returns "unsure" for exactly this case, and nothing acts on "unsure". Then the stranger's mailboxes become "verified" contacts, paid lookups run on the wrong domain, and a pitch is drafted. This is the Acadian/Corvex failure class.
- **The pitch prompt contradicts itself:** "NO NUMBERS… not industry benchmarks either" vs "NUMBERS: only from benchmarks"; "never name a client" vs "Did this for {client}" (`claude-composer.ts:359/640, 596/393`). The capabilities block offers "SnapPad" (a client) and S.P.A.M. itself as proof (`runna/capabilities.ts:31-32`).
- **Silent template fallbacks break every rule:** "Hi there", em dashes, unsourced "70% of leads", "Heading into 2026" (`industry-templates.ts`). They run whenever Claude fails or the cap is hit. The follow-up fallback is English-only. It hasn't fired in practice (all 78 Spanish follow-ups went out in Spanish), but it would.
- **Chain filter deletes real SMBs:** "McDonald Plumbing & Heating", "Desjardins Plomberie", "Wendy's Hair Studio", "Harvey's Auto Body" and "Liverpool Pub" all match (`chain-filter.ts:24-52`, run by the audit agent).
- **Scoring is partly blind.** Industry fit is circular (industry = the search keyword). Employee size is never set automatically (15 points always guessed). The contact signal and actual pain text never reach the scorer. No temperature, so scores near the 50/70 cutoffs are noisy. Triage thresholds differ between pipeline (50/70) and bulk re-score (40).
- **Pipeline holes.** Prospects that time out after scraping are stranded as `researched` forever (29 today). The catch-up pitching phase can pitch prospects that skipped enrichment/triage. The prune phase deletes CSV uploads. Inbound Hunter leads get a −10 red flag.
- **Places returns at most 20 per keyword** (no pagination) → re-running discovery returns the same companies. Daily automated discovery would starve.
- **Warmup placement check never matches** (stores the Gmail API id, searches IMAP by Message-ID; `warmup/imap.ts:93`). So the spam-rate auto-pause and placement-based re-warm can never fire.
- **Deliverability is disconnected from cold sending.** Warmup pauses write `warmup_config.status`; send paths only read `sender_inboxes.paused`, which only the settings form sets. `bounce_rate_7d` / `spam_rate_7d` columns exist and are never written.
- **No timezone, weekday or blackout handling.** Cold email goes out at 15:xx UTC seven days a week; `blackout_dates` is never checked.
- **Analytics mislead:** reply rate divides by pitch *rows* (understated up to 3×); outcomes are attributed to what the *score* suggested, not what the pitch used; `claudeGenerated` and `perPitchUsd` are always 0/null.
- **Booked/won prospects can be bulk-deleted** (the guard checks `opportunities`, which is never written; deletion cascades replies + pitches).
- **OAuth callback** doesn't check the connected Google account matches the inbox, and `state` has no nonce. Connecting the wrong account would send cold mail from it.
- **Unauthenticated `verify-email` edge function** (`verify_jwt=false`, CORS `*`, no secret, no list limit). Probes identify as runnareach.com.

## 4. Constraints that shape the design

1. **Volume vs learning.** Today's cold capacity is **6 first emails a day** (`SEND_PER_TICK = 6`), one inbox (cap 30/day shared with follow-ups and nudges). At a 2.2% reply rate that's ~4 replies a month. A classic A/B test needs about **1,000 first emails per variant** to detect a reply-rate doubling (2.2% → 4.4%), and about **4,000 per variant** for a doubling of *positive* replies. So "learn from responses" cannot mean A/B testing on replies at this scale; it would take years. The loop has to learn from **denser signals** (§6.1) and qualitative review, and volume has to grow.
2. **Vercel Hobby** fires each cron once a day at a fixed random minute, with a 60–120s function cap. You can't pace sends across business hours or poll replies often. Separately, Vercel's Hobby terms are for personal, non-commercial use, which a revenue-generating outbound engine isn't. Pro ($20/mo) fixes both: per-minute crons and longer functions. The alternative is Supabase `pg_cron` + `pg_net` (available, not installed) calling the routes on a schedule.
3. **Everything assumes a logged-in human.** Every stage calls `requireUser()` and the cookie client. Unattended runs need a **system actor** (service-role client + tenant + actor id + `CRON_SECRET` entry points) threaded through discovery, research, enrichment and pitch generation. This is the biggest single refactor.
4. **One sending domain.** Every mistake lands on `runnareach.com`. Automation must protect it first and grow volume second.

## 5. Target design: "Autopilot"

### 5.1 Principles

1. **Nothing sends unless every check passes.** Deterministic rules are law; an AI critic is a second opinion; you handle exceptions. Failing pitches go to a review queue, never to a fallback that sends.
2. **Autonomy is earned per stage, with numbers.** Shadow mode, then a veto window, then full auto, each promotion gated on measured agreement with your own decisions.
3. **Safety rules are not learnable.** The learning loop can change *what we say and to whom*, never the gate, compliance, caps or circuit breakers.
4. **Learn from dense signals first,** sparse ones (meetings) last.
5. **Every decision is recorded immutably,** or it can't be learned from.

### 5.2 The daily loop

```
 06:00 local  DISCOVER   rotate ICPs by learned yield; paginate Places; city tiling;
                         budget = what tomorrow's send capacity can absorb
              RESEARCH → SCORE → TRIAGE (existing chain, run as system actor)
              VERIFY     real verifier API on every candidate address (valid / catch-all / invalid)
              COMPOSE    Claude, prompt version from registry, arm chosen by bandit
              GATE A/B/C recipient · content rules · AI critic  →  auto-revise ≤2×
              ROUTE      AUTO lane │ REVIEW lane (you) │ DROP
 business hrs SEND       paced 1-2 per 10 min in the prospect's local 9-11:30 / 14-16:30, weekdays,
                         blackout-aware; just-in-time re-check before EACH send
 every 15 min REPLIES    poll → classify (+confidence) → automated action or alert
 continuous   BREAKERS   bounce / opt-out / DMARC / API errors → auto-pause + alert
 weekly       LEARN      review replies, edits, holds → proposals → you approve → challenger
```

### 5.3 "No shitty emails": three gates plus a just-in-time check

**Gate A: recipient** (must pass all):
- Verified by a real verification API: `valid` sends; `catch-all` goes to the REVIEW lane only; `invalid`/`unknown` is dropped. At about half a cent per check (per the June lessons note), this is the cheapest fix in the plan.
- The domain is confirmed as the prospect's: "unsure" ownership goes to REVIEW. Also: not on do-not-contact, not previously emailed (by address *or* domain), no open sequence.
- Recipient fit: a named decision-maker, or a role inbox at a company small enough that the owner reads it (the data supports `info@` at SMBs). Never `volunteer@`, `careers@`, `privacy@`, `noreply@`.

**Gate B: content rules** (deterministic, extended from today's `send-gate.ts`):
- Today's 8 rules, plus **every** number (all matches, spelled-out numbers, and subjects) must trace to stored evidence or an active benchmark.
- Greeting matches the recipient in both languages (including "Estimado/a"); company name matches `site_name`; any claim about their site requires a successful fetch.
- Language and register consistency (no Spanglish, tú/usted as chosen); no client names; banned-jargon list as a **hard** fail, not a score ding.
- Subject: no em dash, no number unless sourced, length bounds. CTA present, rendering correctly, localized.
- Unsubscribe line + mailing address present.

**Gate C: AI critic.** A separate Claude call, not the writer grading itself. It sees the research evidence and the email and scores a fixed rubric:
- factual grounding: every claim traceable to the evidence;
- recipient appropriateness;
- would a busy owner find it relevant in 5 seconds;
- tone (plain-spoken, not agency);
- Spanish quality judged as native Spanish, not translation.

It returns pass/fail with reasons. On fail, the reasons go back to the writer for up to 2 revisions; still failing goes to REVIEW.

**Just-in-time check**, run immediately before *each* send (cold, follow-up and nudge):
- re-run gate A+B on the exact body and recipient going out;
- read the Gmail thread for any reply since the last poll;
- re-check do-not-contact, prospect status and inbox health.

This closes the reply-race and double-send holes structurally.

**Lanes:**
- **AUTO:** all gates pass, critic ≥ threshold, segment not on probation, inbox healthy.
- **REVIEW:** anything else that's salvageable; shown to you with the failure reasons.
- **DROP:** unverifiable recipient or no evidence.

Templates never enter AUTO.

### 5.4 Sending and circuit breakers

| Signal | Threshold (start conservative) | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Hard bounces | ≥2 in last 50 sends from an inbox (4%) | pause that inbox, alert |
| Hard bounces, global | ≥3 in a day | pause all cold sending, alert |
| Opt-out / hard_no | 3× the 30-day baseline in a week | pause the segment, alert |
| DMARC pass rate | < 90% over 3 days | pause all, alert |
| Blocklist (correctly classified) | any listing | pause all, alert |
| Gmail API | 4xx/5xx, token failure | pause inbox, alert |
| Warmup placement (once fixed) | inbox rate < 80% | pause cold, re-warm |

The breakers write `sender_inboxes.paused` + `paused_reason` (the field the send paths already respect). **Resuming is always manual.** Alerts go to email/push the same day, not in a digest.

### 5.5 Replies

| Intent | Automated action | Human? |
|---|---|---|
| bounced | suppress prospect + DNC the address (fix the overwrite) | no |
| unsubscribe / hard_no | suppress + DNC immediately, no reply | no |
| auto_reply / OOO | parse the return date, reschedule the sequence (fix the permanent pause) | no |
| not_now | snooze to the stated date or 90 days; no nudge | no |
| wrong_person | extract the referred contact → draft for them | **you approve** |
| wants_info / wants_meeting | **instant alert** + drafted reply with booking link | **you send** (at least until trusted) |
| unclassified / low confidence / angry | alert | **you** |

Positive replies are where the money is, and the volume is tiny (1 so far). Keep yourself in that loop. Automate everything around it.

### 5.6 Outcomes

Wire a booking link (Cal.com or similar) with a webhook → `opportunities` row (meeting booked → held → proposal → won/lost + value + loss reason). The table already exists and is never written. Without this, the system can't learn what actually makes money, only what gets replies.

### 5.7 Earning autonomy

| Stage | What happens | Promote when |
|---|---|---|
| **Shadow** | Autopilot labels every pitch AUTO/REVIEW/DROP; you still approve everything. | ≥ 2 weeks and ≥ 100 pitches; ≥ 90% of AUTO-labelled pitches you approved **unedited**; **zero** AUTO pitches you'd have rejected for a factual/recipient error |
| **Veto window** | AUTO pitches are scheduled 12–24h out; a daily digest lets you hold any with one click. | ≥ 3 weeks; veto rate < 5%; bounce < 2%; no breaker trips caused by AUTO |
| **Full auto** | AUTO lane sends directly; REVIEW lane still comes to you. | ongoing: any breaker trip or a factual-error veto drops back a stage automatically |

## 6. The learning loop

### 6.1 What it can learn from (densest first)

| Signal | Volume | Latency | Teaches |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gate + critic verdicts | every pitch | instant | what the writer gets wrong, by segment |
| **Your edits and rejections** | 24% of pitches today | instant | your taste, directly (diff original vs edited) |
| Bounces / verifier results | every send | 1–3 days | which contact sources are trustworthy |
| **Audit-tool runs** (needs attribution) | ~1–2% of sends (1 real run so far) | hours | engagement: *this is the GYMVMT path* |
| Human replies + intent | ~2% | days | message-market fit |
| Meetings / deals | ? (not recorded) | weeks | what actually makes money |

### 6.2 Foundations (must exist before any learning)

- **A decision record on every pitch** (immutable JSONB):
  - prompt version and model; path (claude/template/website/rewrite); fallback reason;
  - angle / CTA / subject-engine arm; benchmarks and case study cited;
  - recipient type and verifier verdict; language and register; normalized segment (industry taxonomy, not the free-text keyword; size band; market);
  - gate verdict, critic score and reasons; edit distance to what was sent.
- **Attribution token** in the audit-tool URL (`&r=<short pitch code>`), stored by runna-hunter on each scan. This needs a change in the runna-hunter repo, and it turns the strongest known engagement signal into a measurable one.
- **Immutable history:** stop overwriting `sequence_step`, intents and sent reply bodies; keep a `pitch_events` row per send/reply/scan/meeting (the table exists, unused).
- **Prompts loaded from the registry** (`prompts` / `prompt_variants`, already in the schema), with `prompt_variant_id` stamped on every pitch. One prompt with no contradictions.

### 6.3 Four mechanisms, in the order they become useful

1. **Weekly learning review** (useful from week 1). A scheduled Claude job reads the week's replies (all intents), your edit diffs, gate/critic failures and vetoes. It writes concrete proposals into `prompt_change_proposals`, each with evidence ("6 of 9 edits removed the second paragraph"; "both hard-no's objected to X"). You approve or reject on /learning. Approved changes become a **challenger** prompt version. This is how a low-volume system actually improves: qualitative, evidence-cited, human-approved.
2. **Exemplar memory** (useful from ~20 strong examples). Emails that earned a positive reply or audit run, and your hand-edited finals, are embedded (pgvector is installed) and retrieved as few-shot examples for similar prospects. Rejected and vetoed emails become negative examples for the critic.
3. **Bandit over a few discrete arms** (useful at ~1,000+ sends/month). Thompson sampling across 3–4 options on *one* dimension at a time (e.g. CTA: audit tool / 15-min call / reply-one-word), reward = audit run (weight 3) + positive reply (10) + neutral reply (1) − opt-out (5). Keep 15–20% on the current champion as a control. It shifts traffic toward winners gradually, without needing "statistical significance".
4. **Targeting** (useful at ~30 positives). ICP rotation weighted by positive-engagement yield per prospect researched; retire ICPs with near-total suppression (Ontario/BC Professional Services). Only then recalibrate the score weights against outcomes. Not before: with 1 positive, any fitted model would be noise.

### 6.4 What learning may never touch

Gate rules, verifier policy, compliance footer, caps, circuit breakers, do-not-contact, the "no invented numbers" rule. All prompt changes are human-approved at first. Auto-promotion of a challenger is a later, explicit decision.

## 7. Roadmap

## 11. Addendum: the near-free version (Pedro's cost rule)

### What a paid verifier does, and why we won't copy it

Services like ZeroBounce, NeverBounce and MillionVerifier connect to the recipient's mail server and ask "would you accept mail for this address?" without sending anything (an SMTP `RCPT` probe). They do it from large IP pools whose reputation they maintain full-time, and they add catch-all detection and databases of known bad addresses.

Doing that ourselves needs a server with outbound port 25 open (Vercel and Supabase block it), and that server isn't free. Mail providers treat repeated probing as hostile, so that server's IP gets blocklisted. And Google/Microsoft-hosted domains, where most SMBs live, often accept every probe anyway, so the answer is unreliable exactly where we need it.

We copy what these tools *achieve*, using our own data, not their mechanism. We also don't tap paid platforms' private APIs or scrape their products: that breaks their terms and breaks without warning.

### What our 26 bounces were (prod data, Jun 24 – Aug 8)

| Cause | Count | Free fix |
|---|---|---|
| Junk text, not a real address (glued words, phone numbers, placeholders like `ejemplo@misitio.com`) | 15 | strict format check + DNS (domain resolves and has MX) |
| Wrong company's domain (`asi…@asicentral.com`, a directory) | 1 | address domain must equal the prospect's own site domain |
| Real domain, mailbox doesn't exist | 7 | provenance + freshness rules below |
| Recipient policy block (incl. a Claude-guessed address) | 2 | never auto-send guesses |
| Temporary delay misread as a bounce | 1 | bug: "Delivery incomplete" is not a bounce; don't suppress on it |

All the junk predates the Jul 21 extractor fix. The 4 bounces since then are real-domain/missing-mailbox or policy cases.

### "SPAM Verify": our own verifier, $0

Each candidate address gets a verdict (`send` / `review` / `drop`) and the reasons, stored on the contact:

1. **Format** (have it; tighten): reject any TLD not in the public suffix list, digits or labels glued to the local part, placeholders, and system inboxes (noreply/privacy/careers/volunteer).
2. **DNS** (free, works on Vercel): domain resolves, has MX (or A fallback), is not a null-MX, not parked. Cache results per domain.
3. **Ownership:** the address domain must equal the prospect's site domain (or a known alias from its own site). "Unsure" ownership goes to review.
4. **Provenance:**
   - **High:** published as a `mailto:` on the company's own site.
   - **Medium:** a verified finder result.
   - **Low:** a pattern guess or a Claude pick.
   - Low never auto-sends.
5. **Freshness, just in time:** re-fetch the page the address came from right before the first email. If it's no longer published there, don't send. This catches departed staff (`hberry@`) and retired inboxes (`info@woodbinehotels.com`) for free.
6. **Our own outcome memory:**
   - Every bounce goes to DNC (fix the overwrite bug).
   - A domain that hard-bounced blocks all non-published addresses at that domain.
   - Each source's bounce rate (scraper / finder / manual / engine) feeds the next verdict.
7. **Bounce budget:** at most 1 address per day with less than high provenance until the 50-send rolling bounce rate is under 2%. The circuit breaker (§5.4) backs this up.

**Optional, zero new vendors:** if the existing Hunter account's plan includes email verifications, use its verifier on medium-provenance addresses only, within the quota. I'll check the quota via its API before relying on it.
